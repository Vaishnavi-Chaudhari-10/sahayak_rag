Traditional medical knowledge, such as the medicinal use of 
herbs, is often associated with genetic resources. For instance, 
calanolides, compounds derived from the latex of Calophyllum 
trees found in the Malaysian rainforest, are a potential treatment 
for HIV and certain types of cancer. Because genetic resources 
exist in nature and are not creations of the human mind, they 
cannot be directly protected as intellectual property (IP). They 
are, however, subject to access and benefit-sharing regulations 
under international agreements. 2 This brief focuses on IP 
protection of traditional medical knowledge and does not deal 
specifically with associated genetic resources.
