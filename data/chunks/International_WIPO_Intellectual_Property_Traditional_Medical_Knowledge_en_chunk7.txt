In the case of captopril, a drug used to treat hypertension and 
heart failure, no benefits have flowed back to the indigenous 
Brazilian tribe that first used pit viper venom as an arrowhead 
poison. By contrast, the San people of the Kalahari Desert 
have a benefit-sharing agreement with South Africa’s Council 
for Scientific and Industrial Research, which is working with 
pharmaceutical companies to develop dietary supplements 
based on hoodia, a succulent plant well-known to the San for its 
appetite-suppressant qualities.
