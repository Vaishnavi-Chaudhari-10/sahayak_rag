But, in other cases, such as in respect of granted patents, the 
courts generally possess adequate authority and capacity to 
require testimony and the production of documents that may 
be needed to determine good faith or fraudulent intention. If 
the patent holder proves that they have acted in good faith 
or that the failure to comply was due to a cause outside their 
control and which could not be reasonably foreseen, the court 
may decide that, absent any fraudulent behavior, they should 
not face sanctions for that non-compliance.
