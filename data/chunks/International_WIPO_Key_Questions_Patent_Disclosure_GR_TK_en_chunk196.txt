If there is no explicit ground for opposition under 
national law, the validity of a patent may not easily be 
challenged by TK holders and indigenous communities 
once it has been granted. Complex foreign legal rules 
(such as legal standing for opposition, the burden of 
proof and a strong presumption of patent validity) 
and the high costs of taking legal action – possibly 
in a foreign jurisdiction – may also prevent them from 
taking further action. In sum, the construction of legal 
standing rights determines who may have access to the 
remedies available to redress the harm caused by an 
alleged misappropriation of GRs and/or TK (as defined 
under national law) or an erroneously granted patent.
