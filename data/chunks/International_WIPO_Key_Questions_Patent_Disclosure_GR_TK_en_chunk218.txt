The Nagoya Protocol reaffirms that GRs are subject 
to national sovereignty11 and offers the opportunity 
to recognize the rights of indigenous and local 
communities over their GRs12 and associated TK.13 
The Nagoya Protocol thus responds not only to 
the concerns of the countries of origin of GRs, but 
also to those of the user countries, in that it aims 
to establish a clear and transparent framework for 
access to GRs.14 The main content of the Protocol 
comprises four interrelated pillars: access to 
GRs, benefit-sharing, TK and compliance.
